Contingency agencies vs ConnectPath: which should you use?

We are obviously not neutral here. But a comparison that pretends the other option has no upside is useless, so here is the honest version, including when a contingency agency is genuinely the better call.

Both models exist to solve the same problem: you need a senior person and finding them yourself is slow. They just go about it very differently, and the difference matters more at some stages than others.

Where contingency agencies actually win

Credit where it is due. Contingency recruiting has real strengths:

  • You pay nothing until you hire. If you never make an offer, you never pay a fee. For a company that is unsure whether it will hire at all, that is a low-commitment way to start.
  • You can run several at once. Nothing stops you from briefing three agencies on the same role and taking whoever delivers. For a very common, high-volume role, that breadth can work in your favor.
  • Deep benches in specific niches. Some agencies have spent a decade in one vertical and genuinely know everyone in it.

If you are hiring a common role, you are fine doing the screening yourself, and you want zero upfront commitment, a good contingency agency is a reasonable choice. We will say that plainly.

Where the embedded model pulls ahead

The trouble is that most startup hires are not common, high-volume roles. They are a specific senior engineer or a first leadership hire, where a bad fit is expensive and speed matters. That is where the ConnectPath model was built to win:

  • The fee is flat. $25,000 per search, or $40,000 for leadership roles above $400k, no matter the salary. The person advising you on comp does not profit from a higher number.
  • You get a short list, not a pile. Every candidate is screened and interviewed against your rubric before you see them, so your time goes to interviews that matter.
  • We learn your team. Time inside your company means fit is designed in, not left to chance.
  • It is guaranteed. Candidates in fourteen days, a hire in thirty or your money back, and a free ninety-day replacement. A contingency agency rarely puts its fee at risk that way.

How to actually decide

Skip the sales pitch and answer three questions. First, is this role common or specialized? Specialized points to embedded. Second, how expensive is a bad hire? If a wrong senior engineer sets you back a quarter, you want vetting and fit, not volume. Third, do you have time to screen resumes yourself? If the honest answer is no, paying to move that work off your plate is the entire point.

For a founder hiring senior engineers and leadership at an early-stage, venture-backed company, the embedded flat-fee model wins on almost every axis that matters. For a high-volume, lower-stakes role where you want to pay only on success, a contingency agency is a fair pick. Choose based on the role in front of you, not the logo on the invoice.
